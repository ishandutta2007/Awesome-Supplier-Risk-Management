Curated List of SaaS Products & Open-Source GitHub Projects
Focused on Supplier Risk, Third-Party Risk, Supply Chain Resilience, Supplier Intelligence & Continuous Monitoring
Last updated: August 2026

This repository tracks notable SaaS/hosted platforms and open-source projects for Supplier Risk Management. These tools help organizations identify, assess, monitor, and mitigate risks across suppliers, vendors, third parties, and extended supply networks.

Supplier risk management covers areas including supplier discovery, onboarding, inherent-risk assessment, financial risk, cyber risk, operational risk, geopolitical risk, ESG and sustainability risk, sanctions and compliance screening, adverse media, supply-chain mapping, concentration risk, disruption monitoring, resilience planning, questionnaires, assessments, remediation, and continuous supplier monitoring.

Examples include Interos, Prewave, Everstream Analytics, RapidRatings, Resilinc, IntegrityNext, Source Intelligence, Assent, Sphera, and OneTrust Third-Party Risk — alongside other major supplier-risk, supply-chain-intelligence, ESG, and third-party-risk platforms.

The commercial market increasingly emphasizes continuous monitoring, multitier supply-chain mapping, risk-event intelligence, financial intelligence, regulatory/ESG risk, and automated risk response, rather than periodic supplier questionnaires alone.

Open-source emphasis: The open-source ecosystem is considerably smaller than the commercial supplier-risk market. Therefore, this list expands beyond complete supplier-risk platforms to include open-source TPRM systems, supplier-risk frameworks, GRC platforms, risk intelligence components, OSINT tools, security-rating infrastructure, workflow engines, graph databases, compliance frameworks, and data-processing building blocks that can be combined into a self-hosted supplier-risk stack.

Open-Source GitHub Projects
Complete / Near-Complete TPRM Platforms

LT-VRM
Open-source, self-hosted Third-Party Risk Management / Vendor Risk Management platform covering vendor onboarding, assessments, risk scoring, security questionnaires, contracts, breach and dark-web monitoring, remediation, and vendor portals. It is MIT licensed and designed to run on PHP/MySQL/XAMPP.

OpenVendor
Open-source TPRM initiative focused on democratizing third-party risk management, including vendor evaluation and risk guidance.

Risk Ledger
Open-source/API-oriented project associated with the Risk Ledger third-party and supply-chain risk management model, enabling organizations to manage supplier risk information and relationships.

TPRM Frameworks & Data Models

TPRM Framework
Open framework for third-party risk management covering object models, workflows, data flows, inherent risk, quality of risk management, and residual risk. Useful as a conceptual foundation for building a custom supplier-risk platform.

Third-Party Risk Assessment
Open-source-oriented third-party risk assessment framework covering vendor security evaluation, inherent risk scoring, compliance mapping, and remediation governance.

OpenSSF
Collection of open-source software supply-chain security projects that can be used to evaluate the cybersecurity risk of technology suppliers and software vendors.

Open-source GRC platform supporting risk management, compliance, controls, policies, assessments, and related governance processes. It can be adapted for third-party risk programs.

SimpleRisk
Open-source risk-management platform ecosystem that can be extended for supplier and third-party risk workflows.

CISO Assistant
Open-source GRC platform supporting risk assessment, compliance frameworks, controls, evidence, and organizational risk management. Useful as a foundation for TPRM governance.

OpenGRC
Open-source GRC project for managing governance, risk, compliance, policies, controls, and assessments.

MISP

Typical Open-Source Supplier Risk Architecture

A company seeking to build a self-hosted alternative to commercial supplier-risk platforms can combine:

LT-VRM / custom TPRM application — supplier onboarding, questionnaires, assessments, risk scoring, contracts, remediation, and lifecycle management.

CISO Assistant / Eramba — governance, controls, compliance frameworks, and enterprise risk management.

Neo4j / Apache AGE — model supplier ownership, relationships, dependencies, fourth parties, locations, and concentration.

SpiderFoot / Amass — collect external intelligence and digital-footprint information.

OpenCTI + MISP — integrate cybersecurity threat intelligence.

Nuclei + OpenVAS — assess technical exposure where appropriate and authorized.

OpenSearch — index adverse media, supplier events, documents, and intelligence.

ClickHouse — process large volumes of supplier events and historical risk signals.

PostgreSQL — maintain supplier master data, assessments, evidence, controls, and scores.

n8n / Node-RED — automate supplier onboarding, reminders, alerts, and integrations.

Airflow / Temporal — orchestrate recurring monitoring and assessment workflows.

Kafka — stream continuous supplier-risk events.

Grafana — visualize supplier risk, concentration, events, and remediation.

MinIO — self-host evidence, certificates, questionnaires, contracts, and supplier documents.

Keycloak — provide identity, SSO, and role-based access.

This architecture can create a self-hosted supplier-risk platform spanning:

Supplier → Onboarding → Inherent Risk → Assessment → Intelligence → Continuous Monitoring → Event Detection → Risk Scoring → Remediation → Residual Risk → Offboarding

How to Contribute

Fork the repo.

Add/edit entries in README.md following the existing format.

Include: name, link, 1–2 sentence description, and whether it is SaaS, hosted, open-source, open-core, or source-available.

For GitHub projects, prefer the canonical upstream repository.

Clearly distinguish complete supplier-risk platforms from GRC systems, OSINT tools, cyber-risk tools, graph databases, and supporting infrastructure.

Verify the current license and repository activity before labeling a project as open source.

Mention the principal risk domains supported by each platform.

Avoid describing generic GRC or cybersecurity tools as complete supplier-risk platforms unless they have meaningful TPRM functionality.

Submit a PR with a short explanation.

Star the repo if you find it useful!

Disclaimer

This is a community-curated list — not exhaustive and not an endorsement.

Commercial supplier-risk platforms frequently change ownership, product names, packaging, and capabilities.

Supplier risk management, third-party risk management, GRC, cybersecurity ratings, supply-chain intelligence, ESG platforms, and procurement systems are related but distinct categories.

The open-source ecosystem is substantially smaller than the commercial supplier-risk ecosystem.

Some projects listed in the open-source section are building blocks rather than complete production-grade supplier-risk platforms.

Always verify the current license before deploying or redistributing software.

Open source, source-available, free software, and free hosted tiers are different concepts.

Commercial platforms may have proprietary datasets, entity-resolution systems, financial intelligence, adverse-media feeds, supplier graphs, AI models, and analyst-generated intelligence that cannot be replicated simply by deploying open-source software.

A self-hosted supplier-risk platform requires significant work around data acquisition, entity resolution, data quality, intelligence normalization, risk models, alerting, workflow design, security, privacy, and regulatory compliance.

Supplier monitoring should be performed using lawful and appropriately licensed data sources.

Technical security assessment of suppliers should only be performed with appropriate authorization.

Risk scores should remain explainable and should not be treated as absolute representations of supplier safety.

Critical suppliers should generally be evaluated using multiple independent evidence sources rather than relying on a single automated score.
